# Two consultants, one pattern: AIRE and Land Sterling vs Solum

*Reading the two consulting proposals side by side, then against Solum. The point is
the convergence. When two firms with opposite value propositions structure the same
job the same way, that structure is signal, not coincidence.*

## The two consultants are opposites, and that is the useful part

| | AIRE | Land Sterling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | AI-software-led consulting | RICS valuation house |
| The pitch | Proprietary data and AI | Chartered credibility and bank acceptance |
| Fee | AED 20,000 | AED 73,500 |
| Timeline | 3 weeks | 10-12 working days |
| Data basis | Proprietary MEA datasets (claimed) | Public data plus expert interviews (stated) |
| Method | 4 software-run tasks | 7 human-led stages |
| Credibility anchor | In5 incubator, project track record | RICS/IVSC/CIOB, PI cover, 30+ banks accept |
| Model handed over | No | No |

One sells technology, the other sells trust. The cheaper one is slower. The pricier
one is faster and leans entirely on chartered sign-off. They agree on almost nothing
about why a client should hire them. That is what makes their agreements worth
listening to.

## Where they converge, and what each convergence tells Solum

**1. Both put Highest & Best Use at the centre.** AIRE runs it as a software
optimiser across asset classes. Land Sterling runs it as the textbook four-test
(legally permissible, physically developable, financially feasible, maximally
productive). Different mechanics, same conclusion: the real question for a land buyer
is not "does my scheme work" but "what should go here." Two firms with opposite
models both lead with it. This is the strongest possible signal for Solum's number
one learning. Solum answers the narrower question today. The scoped, residential-only
version of HBU (sell vs hold, configuration, positioning, tier) is the right first
step toward the question both consultants charge to answer.

**2. Both hide the financial model.** AIRE withholds the Excel. Land Sterling states
the model is "not a deliverable" and only high-level to EBITDA. The client pays tens
of thousands and never gets the working model, only a PDF of its outputs. Solum is
the inverse: a live, inspectable, re-runnable model the developer drives directly.
That is not a small nicety. It is a category difference, and Solum should say so out
loud. The thing consultants guard is the thing Solum gives away.

**3. Both structure the study the same way.** Strip the branding and both are: site
and planning envelope, market and competitor evidence, best-use call, massing and
unit mix, financial feasibility with sensitivity, risk and recommendation. Solum
already does the envelope (DDA), the comps (DLD), the unit-mix optimisation, and the
financial feasibility. The two stages Solum is thinnest on are the same two both
consultants spend real labour on: forward-looking market and competitor analysis
(pipeline, absorption, velocity, payment plans) and the explicit best-use call.
Solum's coming-soon market layers target the first. A light HBU view would open the
second.

**4. Neither has a genuine live-data moat.** AIRE claims proprietary datasets. Land
Sterling openly uses public data and interviews. Solum's direct DDA plot lookup and
direct DLD transaction feed is a real, wired-in official-data advantage that neither
consultant demonstrates. The market-analysis stage both firms bill by hand is
exactly the manual work Solum's data integration is positioned to automate.

## What the pricing spread says about where Solum plays

AED 20k to AED 73,500 for the same study, depending on whose signature and whose data
story you buy. Both take one to three weeks. Neither can be run across a pipeline of
plots to decide which one to bid on, because at AED 20k-plus and multi-week
turnaround you commission a study only after you have already shortlisted.

That is the gap Solum owns. Solum is the layer that runs on every plot, instantly, to
decide which one is even worth a consultant. The consultants are the deep dive on the
one plot that survives. Solum does not compete with the AED 73,500 signed opinion. It
decides whether that opinion is worth buying, and for which plot. The two are
sequential, not rival.

The longer-run move, once the screening layer is trusted, is for Solum to generate
the first draft of the study itself, collapsing the one-to-three-week deliverable
into an export. That is where the earlier AIRE analysis and this one meet.

## Net effect on the Solum roadmap

Nothing here changes the ranked priorities from the AIRE analysis. It sharpens the
conviction behind the top two.

- **Light Highest & Best Use is now the clearest bet.** Two independent consultants,
  opposite business models, both lead with it. Build the residential-scoped version.
- **Forward-looking market and competitor data is the labour both consultants sell.**
  Automating it (the coming-soon supply, off-plan, and absorption layers) attacks the
  most expensive part of their studies with Solum's real data advantage.
- **Make the live model a stated feature.** Both consultants withhold theirs. Solum's
  is open and re-runnable. That is a selling point, not just an implementation detail.
- **Trust is the consultants' real moat, and it is not code.** Land Sterling's RICS
  and bank acceptance is unbuyable with software. Solum should not pretend to replace
  the signed valuation. It should own the fast screen ahead of it, and package its own
  honesty (sourced fields, labelled estimates, an inspectable model) as the trust it
  can actually offer.
